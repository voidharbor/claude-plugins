# Registers

Read one block. The one that matches the reader named in step 1. Applying more than one
is what collapses every surface into a single voice.

Length caps are defaults. If the user's own samples run shorter, theirs win.

---

## Correspondence: someone the writer knows

- Length cap: 60 words.
- Open with the thing, not with pleasantries.
- Sign-off: initials or first name, or nothing at all in a running thread.
- One subject, one ask, stop.
- Warmth markers (exclamation marks, thanks) belong here if the user uses them.

---

## Correspondence: a business they do not know yet

Suppliers, agencies, government offices, a shop found online.

- Length cap: 90 words.
- Establish who they are in one clause, not a paragraph.
- The ask goes in the same paragraph as the introduction.
- Never explain the writer's business model, volume, or other ventures.
- One breath of why-you, then move on. See the surveillance section of `tells.md`.

---

## Correspondence: a customer or client

- Length cap: 200 words. The only correspondence register that runs long, because it
  carries findings, prices, or bad news.
- Full sentences, no abbreviations.
- Bullets are acceptable here and only here, for physical findings or line items. Never
  for arguments or reasoning.
- State cost exposure plainly and early, not buried at the end.
- Do not soften a real problem into a positive.

---

## Correspondence: informal, someone close

- Length cap: 50 words.
- Dropped apostrophes and abbreviations are in range if the user writes that way.
- No sign-off, no structure.

---

## Marketing and web copy

- Sentences a person would say out loud to a customer standing in front of them.
- No superlatives that cannot be checked. No "premier", "trusted", "leading".
- Say what the company does and what the next step costs. Nothing else.
- Length is set by the page slot, not by this file. Fill the slot and stop.
- This is the one register where a headline may be crafted. Body copy still may not.

---

## Social and short-form video

- Read the room of the specific platform or subreddit first. There is no house voice.
- No hype adjectives, no emoji strings, no "let's dive in".
- Titles state what the thing is. They do not tease.
- Keep personal accounts and business accounts in separate voices.

---

## Docs, READMEs, commit messages

- Plain technical description. No voice at all.
- Commit messages: one line, what changed. No body unless unavoidable.
- READMEs: what it is, how to run it, what breaks. No marketing tone, no emoji headers.
- This register uses no voice reference. There is nothing personal to match.

---

## Chat replies to the user

Not a document, but it is text a person reads, so the hard bans apply.

- No em dashes. This is where they leak most often.
- Lead with the answer or the finding.
- Structure is allowed and useful here, unlike in email.
- Do not perform enthusiasm about the work.
