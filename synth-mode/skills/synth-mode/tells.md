# Kill list

Phrases and constructions that mark text as machine-made. Delete on sight. This list is
for removal only. Nothing here is a suggestion about what to write instead.

## Self-referential framing

Text that narrates its own existence. A person writing an email does not describe the
email.

- "That's the whole email"
- "I'll keep this short"
- "Short answer first"
- "Two things worth flagging"
- "Here's the thing"
- "To be clear" / "To be direct"
- "Let me be honest with you"
- Any sentence announcing what the next paragraph will do

## Sales throat-clearing

- "I'm not selling anything" / "not a pitch" / "no strings"
- "genuinely useful to me"
- "Reply 'no thanks' and I won't write again"
- "I'll get straight to the point"
- Any opt-out clause phrased as policy. "No worries if not" is how a person says it.
  Commercial email still carries a postal address in the signature where law requires
  it. That part is law, not style.

## Model vocabulary

- delve, leverage (as a verb), landscape (figurative), seamless, robust, holistic
- game-changer, needle-mover, no-brainer
- "It's worth noting"
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "That said," as a paragraph opener
- "at the end of the day"

## Structural tells

- Rule of three where two examples would do
- A sentence, then its restatement, then its implication
- Bullets inside a message to one person
- Bold on a phrase for emphasis inside an email
- Parallel sentence openings across consecutive paragraphs
- Closing paragraph that summarizes the message above it

## Surveillance personalization

Naming a detail the reader did not tell you, in a way that proves you went looking.

- Bad: "Your contact form asks for the year the house was built."
- Bad: "I saw you've been in business since 2011."
- Fine: "You're one of the only shops around here I could find an email for."

A reason for writing is fine. Proof of research is not.

## Manufactured casualness

The subtlest category, and the one that makes writing sound like it is trying to be
human rather than being written by one.

- Deliberate sentence fragments used for rhythm
- A one-line paragraph placed for impact
- Lowercase openers chosen as a style
- "kind of" or "honestly" inserted to soften a sentence that was already fine
- Any line that would work as a caption or a pull quote

## Not automatically tells

These get flagged as machine writing by reflex, but plenty of people write them
naturally. Check the user's real samples before removing any of them. Deleting someone's
actual habits is as wrong as adding habits they do not have.

- Exclamation marks. Many people use several per message with people they know.
- "Hope you're doing well"
- "Thank you so much"
- "Don't hesitate to reach out"
- "Hey [Name]!" as an opener
